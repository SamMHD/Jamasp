## What happened

**1. Logan didn't want a hold — she wanted a hike. (Bearish, high conviction,
partly realized.)** Her explanation of the FOMC dissent, verbatim from the wire:
*"Would have preferred a quarter-point rate increase"*; *"monetary policy is not
restraining the economy, and inflation is not on course to return to the 2%
goal"*; *"modest Fed action in the near term would reduce the likelihood of
needing sharper action later."* Kashkari and Hammack explained the same day.
Barkin separately called the next decision a *"close call."* Yesterday I said
the hawkish dissent was underpriced. Friday it got priced — 10y to 4.745%, gold
−1.5%. **That call was right and it has now largely paid out.** The remaining
edge in it is thinner than it was 24 hours ago; I am not double-counting it.

**2. UMich final July 55.2 vs 54.0 expected, one-year inflation expectations
eased. (Bearish, medium.)** Removes an expectations prop under the debasement
story. Caveat that cuts the other way: this is a July survey, so it carries the
same borrowed-from-the-ceasefire flattery as June's core PCE — collected before
crude's move. Do not extrapolate it into August.

**3. Trump has ordered preparations to strike Iranian energy infrastructure,
possibly this weekend, with Israel joining for the first time since February.
(Bullish, high conviction on the tail, unknowable on timing.)** Three
independent outlets converge: WSJ says preparations are ordered and could begin
as soon as this weekend; CBS says the US and Israel are planning joint strikes
on energy targets — likely **oil refineries and power plants**; Axios says
internal discussion is focused on infrastructure and energy, no final decision.
Iran's counter-threat is explicit and regional: a senior security official told
Tasnim that Tehran has retaliation plans covering Israeli installations **and US
energy assets across the Middle East.** Mechanism if it lands: crude spike →
haven bid and a second inflation impulse into a Fed that just told you it is not
cutting. **This is the largest asymmetric risk on the book and it sits over a
closed weekend.**

**4. Correction — the blockade runs the other way. (Trims yesterday's bullish
carry.)** Yesterday's stance said *"Iran is now stopping ships in Hormuz."* That
was wrong in direction. CENTCOM's 31 July update: **the US** has blockaded
Iranian ports since 14 July and has rerouted 30 commercial vessels, disabled
two, boarded two, and passed ~30 humanitarian ships. Iran *claims* Hormuz is
closed; CENTCOM calls that false and notes thousands of transits in four months.
Harassment is real — a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile 11nm NE of
Limah, Oman, engine room damaged, unable to manoeuvre — but the strait is
functionally open. The shipping premium exists and is smaller than I implied.
Also relevant to duration: this war began **28 February**; Friday was day 152.

**5. Two consecutive days of gold ignoring its own bullish news. (The important
tell.)** Thursday it absorbed the largest momentum crash on record without being
sold. Friday it absorbed a credible imminent-strike report without being bought.
Combined reading: **the marginal price-setter in gold right now is the rates
desk, not the geopolitics desk.** Near-term that is bearish. It is also
precisely what makes the weekend asymmetric — the war is *not in the price*, so
there is nothing to unwind if it doesn't happen and a full repricing if it does.

**6. Positioning is not stretched. (Structurally supportive.)** CFTC net spec
174,131 as of 28 July, slightly lower on the week. Not a crowded long. That caps
the forced-liquidation downside and leaves room above for a geopolitical bid to
run without immediately hitting profit-taking.

**7. Two new structural demand/plumbing items. (Low conviction, both worth
tracking.)** Tether added **14 tonnes** in Q2 alongside $1.5bn of profit — small
against ~1,200t quarterly global demand, but a new category of price-insensitive
non-central-bank, non-ETF holder; it sits on the constructive side of the
metals-financialization theme that Thursday's stress test downgraded. Separately,
India's **NSE launched Electronic Gold Receipts** to formalise the domestic
market — the first real counter-move against the duty-driven grey-market channel.
Ambiguous for this desk: less smuggled volume, but a larger *documented* market,
and documentation is Dubai's edge.

## Outlook

**Base case: the $4,000–$4,180 range holds, but the distribution has gone
bimodal into the weekend.** The two drivers are pushing opposite ways and
operate on different clocks.

- **Downside is continuous and already working.** Hawkish repricing, 10y at
  4.745%, a dissenting bloc that wants hikes rather than fewer cuts. Next test
  is payrolls, Friday 7 August. KITCO reports Wall Street trisected on gold and
  Main Street bulls in the minority — nobody is leaning long into it.
- **Upside is discrete and unpriced.** Strikes on Iranian refineries and power
  plants, with a stated Iranian intent to answer against Gulf energy assets.
  Friday's close does not contain any of this.

**For the desk, the physical exposure precedes the price exposure.** If Iran
answers strikes against US energy assets in the region, Gulf freight rates, war-
risk insurance, and airfreight routing for metal out of DXB move before the
price finishes moving. That is a logistics question to have answered before
Monday, not a price question.

**Conviction:** medium-high that the range holds *absent strikes*; deliberately
low on direction for Monday specifically, because it is event-dependent rather
than uncertain.

**Where I could be wrong:** I may be over-weighting the strike reporting. Trump
has threatened Iranian infrastructure repeatedly across five months and mostly
hasn't followed through; CBS reports White House aides objected at the cabinet
meeting; the negotiating channel is live (Witkoff, Kushner, Vance, Rubio); and
the Hamas disarmament / Gaza withdrawal deal just landed, which Guterres called
the only good news in recent days. A quiet weekend is at least as likely as a
loud one, and Monday would then open with the Fed story alone and gold lower.
Second caveat: DFII10 is two days stale, so I am reading real yields partly off
a nominal proxy.

**What flips me:**
- **Bearish confirmation:** $4,000 breaks on payrolls or on further hike talk,
  with no war bid. Then the debasement premium is thinner than I have assumed
  and the range floor is fiction.
- **Bullish flip:** strikes land *and* gold holds the gain 48 hours later. A
  one-day spike that retraces is noise; a held gap means the marginal holder
  changed.
