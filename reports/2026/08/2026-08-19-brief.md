## Deep dive — FOMC July minutes (22:45 Dubai)

**The hike bar survived July intact — on paper.** The July 28–29 minutes
keep June's conditional-tightening language fully alive: "many participants
assessed that policy tightening would likely be necessary if inflation did
not decline," and "some" said financial conditions "might not currently be
sufficiently restrictive" to return inflation to 2%. The 9–3 hold is
confirmed, all three dissents (Hammack, Logan, Kashkari — +25bp) arguing a
hike now would "forestall the need for a steeper and potentially more
costly sequence of tightening moves." Almost all members retained the
"will deliver price stability" statement language. Read cold, the bar to
hiking is as low as June's.

**But the text is stale, and the market treated it that way.** Everything
in these minutes predates the soft-data run: −23k July payrolls (−103k
revisions), benign CPI, flat PPI — and June core PCE printed −0.1% m/m
(3.7% y/y). CNBC's post-release read: market pricing has switched to a
hold **until December** — September has been priced out, not in.
investinglive concurs: minutes "are rarely a market-moving event… more so
now, after the soft NFP and CPI reports." No repricing toward September
was visible in the hour after release; gold held its gains through the
hawkish headlines.

**Swing voices: the hawkish energy sits at the periphery, not the Board.**
All three dissents are regional presidents. No governor — not Waller, not
Jefferson — joined them, and Williams held with the majority: the
permanent-voter core stayed with Warsh's patience. Goldman's
inflation-dominant framing (16 Aug) describes the committee's *debate*;
the *votes* that decide September sit with a Board that hasn't moved.
First post-data signal from the core comes at Jackson Hole (opens Fri).

Two curveballs worth logging: Warsh floated cutting the FOMC schedule from
8 meetings a year to 6 (no decision, 2026 unchanged) — a chair signaling
slower, information-denser decision points; and the minutes acknowledge an
intermeeting payments-settlement disruption, crediting ample reserves for
orderly money markets — first official mention of that incident.
